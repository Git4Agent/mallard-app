What the standards do

I checked nine systems against their own guidance pages, or their published source files where a page wouldn't load (Material, Carbon, Atlassian). Every one uses a switch for a setting that applies the moment you flip it, which is how Mallard's settings behave. A checkbox is for choices that wait for a Save or Submit. So the switch stays. What they differ on is how it shows its state:

Where they agree

  • Off is neutral or uncoloured, and On takes the one strong fill (Apple, Material, Fluent, Atlassian). Mallard's Off is a strong mid-brown, 3.6:1 against the card, so both states read as filled. That's direction A.
  • Most add a second cue that isn't colour: a mark in the track (Primer, Atlassian, GitLab, Carbon small, the iOS labels option) or the word (Windows, Primer, Carbon, NN/g). That's B or C.
  • They split on the word. Windows, Primer (before) and Carbon (after) show one word. NN/g shows both and warns that one word can pass for the setting's name. That's C's three sub-options.
  • None of the nine checked uses a two-choice segment for an on/off that applies immediately. D is the outlier.
  • Mallard passes WCAG today (the thumb is 3.6:1 on its track), so this is a clarity fix, not a compliance one. The guidance it breaks is Apple's and Atlassian's: don't show state by colour alone.

Measured, both themes

Read from the computed styles above, not from the token sheet. Fill says On needs Off's track to vanish into the card (under 1.5:1, so it reads hollow) and On's track to stand off it (at least 3:1, so it reads filled). The thumb and mark need 3:1 against their own track (WCAG 1.4.11), and C's word is text, so it needs 4.5:1.

Recommendation · C (A's switch + the word), plus the Theme picker fix

A fixes the defect itself. Off stops being a filled bar, so filled means On, and the thumb's size and position agree with the fill. It passes every gate in both themes and it's CSS only. C adds the word, so the state never rests on a shape alone. The switch is the one control in Mallard that states something without words (the contrast test's own comment says so), and notices, badges and receipts all name their state. Honey as the On fill fails in light (1.80:1 against the card, so a lone On still reads as unfilled). Ink is the colour every checkbox in the app already uses.

Why not B: marks are a real standard (Primer, Atlassian and GitLab draw them by default), and B is a sound second choice. But at 10px they're symbols you have to learn, and they need a 50px track. A word needs no decoding. Why the word on the right: the setting's name is at the row's far left, so a word beside the switch can't pass for it (NN/g's worry), and on the right the receipt can't merge with it ("Saved On"). Both words is NN/g's own layout, and a fair pick if you'd rather follow them. Why not D: it's two buttons for one bit, it moves the ink block left and right (so a selected "Off" is the loudest thing in its row), and it rewrites the six-switch test pins.

The Theme picker selects with honey paper on cream, which measures 1.19:1 (1.40 in dark). That's the same "which one is set?" problem, so give its selected segment the same ink fill (the Ink selection button above).

Plan if you pick C

  1. App.css switch block: A's rules replace the current ones: 40×22, 2px ink edge, hollow Off track, a 10px --text-2 thumb Off, a 14px --accent-contrast thumb on an --accent fill On, plus :disabled (0.45) and hover. --switch-off and --switch-thumb lose their only readers, so both tokens are deleted from both palettes.
  2. SettingsPage.tsx: one SettingSwitch renders <label class="sync-settings-toggle"> + the input + an aria-hidden "On"/"Off". It replaces the three inline inputs and PreferenceSwitch's body. The input keeps role="switch", its class, checked and aria-label.
  3. App.css: .sync-settings-toggle / .sync-settings-state as in C's block. The segment's .active becomes --accent / --accent-contrast.
  4. Gates: css-hygiene "the switch shows which way it is set" measures both states. Off thumb on the card ≥3, On thumb on --accent ≥3, --accent vs card ≥3 (fill says On), the Off word on the card ≥4.5, and the selected segment vs the unselected ones ≥3. settings-page adds one pin for the On/Off word. The six-switch regex holds as it is.
  5. Verify: npm run build and npm run test:frontend-integration, then mount the real page with mockIPC and screenshot it in WebKit, light and dark, with all on and all off.
