Header redesign trail = storage / repo / section / session

0 · Before as it ships today

Four stacked blocks say the project's name three times before a single row of content appears: the titlebar, the h1, the meta row, and the storage card. Push and Pull sit at the bottom of the card, two blocks away from the title.

1 · The trail storage / repo / section — and the session appends

Storage leads: it is the outermost scope, the thing everything below it lives inside, and it is what Push and Pull act on. Then the repo, then the section. Open a session and the trail grows a fourth crumb; the section crumb behind it becomes the way back.

One row replaces the h1, the meta row, the storage card and the section heading. Everything you set once is behind ···.

This one is live. Click a session row to drill in, then click Sessions to come back. Click any crumb with a ⌄ to open its menu.
Drilling in appends; it never replaces. Sessions stops being the leaf and becomes a link back, losing its ⌄ — the section switcher belongs to the section you are on, not one you are inside. Same rule for Git history → a commit and Files → a file.
Only the leaf truncates. Storage, repo and section keep their full width, so the two crumbs that decide what Push writes are never the ones that get shortened.
Typography law held. Crumbs carrying your data — storage name, repo name, session title — stay in --font-sans; only the closed vocabulary (Sessions, Git history, Pull, Push, menu group labels) speaks pixel, which is the rule RetroTheme.css enforces with a test.
