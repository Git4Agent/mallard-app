Left-aligned, using the room

Nothing is pinned to the right in any of these: the badge follows the name, the date follows the login. Below the name, every line starts flush with the glyph, so the card has one left edge and no gutter of dead space. The card on staging leaves its right half empty; these spend that width on words beside the figures, on labels, or on larger type, and each keeps a fixed grid so the same fact lands in the same place on every card. An empty slot shows a dim dash.

G1 Words in the slots the shipped ledger, spelled out

Same three lines as staging, but the figures get their nouns back: "3 sessions", "8 files", "16.4 KiB". Slots are wide enough for the word, so "1 file" and "8 files" start at the same x. Marks lead the row; the footer reads "Rika321 · Sep 14, 2026".

  • Reads without tooltips. A visitor knows what 3 and 8 are.
  • Fills the width. The row ends where the card does.
  • Slot widths are language-bound. Longer nouns would need wider slots.

G2 Stat strip labels over figures, like a dashboard tile

The facts become a strip of small-caps labels with the figure beneath: SESSIONS 3, FILES 8, SIZE 16.4 KiB, PUSHED Sep 14. The marks sit after the name. The label row is what aligns the eye; the figures are bigger because the label has already said what they are.

  • Self-labelling. No icons to decode, no tooltips.
  • Figures line up as a table across the whole row of cards.
  • Four lines again. ~116px per card.

Where I would land

G1. It is the staging card with the nouns restored and the right edge given up, which is exactly the room the card has: the words fill the row, the footer reads as one phrase, and nothing needs a tooltip. G2 if you want the card to feel like a stats tile; G3 if the card should mirror the About rail; G4 if legibility matters more than density.
