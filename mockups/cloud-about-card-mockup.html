The repository info card

Mock only, drawn at the rail's real width in three states: the Owner of a private project, a visitor on a public skill, and a repository with nothing pushed yet.

What the references do: GitHub keeps About to description, link and topic chips, puts counts apart as icon rows, and gives Contributors and Releases their own cards. PyPI splits its rail into small labelled groups and always gives a count its noun. Nielsen Norman: one concept per card, and keep the things people look up as a scannable list rather than prose.

Before · as shipped

One card, one flat list. What the repository is, who the people are, what the latest push is and account facts (stored, fingerprint, your role) all carry the same weight, and the owner appears twice when they also pushed.

1 · One concept per card (GitHub-shaped)

About keeps only what the repository is: description, kind and provider chips, the GitHub link, then counts as icon rows. People and Latest push become their own cards, and identity trivia hides behind a disclosure. Closest to the reference pages; the tallest of the three.

2 · One card, labelled groups (PyPI-shaped)

Still one card, but broken into small labelled groups with the label in mono small caps: Latest push, Repository, Identity. Each group keeps a scannable two-column list, which is what a lookup wants.

3 · Figures first

Borrows the home page's readout: four ink cells carry the numbers people compare (stored, public version, stars, members), then the description, then the short list. The most Mallard of the three, and the least like a form.
