Cards that line up

What went wrong in the shipped card: the name shared its line with the "yours" pill and four marks, so it was cut to "HorrorGa…"; the readouts and the date floated with the content, so no two cards put a figure in the same place; and a card with a known size wrapped to a third line while its neighbours did not.

All three fixes below share the same rules:

  • the name owns its line; nothing else may shorten it;
  • readouts sit in fixed slots (sessions, files, size), empty slots stay empty, so figures align across the row;
  • the date has one place and one width on every card;
  • every card in a grid is the same height, whatever it holds.

D1 Three lines name · marks · figures, four across

Line 1 is the name and the chevron. Line 2 is the marks: providers, the pusher, and a small "yours" label, with the date at the end. Line 3 is the figures in fixed slots. The card is taller, and every one of them is the same height.

  • Nothing crowds the name. Fourteen-character names fit at four across.
  • Three voices, three lines. Identity, people, figures never share a line.
  • Tallest. Three rows show nine cards before scrolling instead of ten.

D2 Two lines, fixed end column the desktop row, with a ledger column

The card keeps two lines, but the end becomes a fixed 104px column for the marks, and the date takes the last slot of the figures row, right-aligned. The name takes everything to its left. "Yours" becomes the glyph's well filled in ink instead of a pill, so it costs no width; the tooltip still spells it out.

  • Shortest card. Two lines, as shipped; ten cards before scrolling.
  • Hard alignment. Marks, date, and figures each have a column; nothing floats.
  • Name gets 130px. Enough for "godot-scene-lint"; longer names still cut.
  • Filled well needs learning. The ink square means "yours" only once you know it.

D3 Three across the shipped card, given room

Same two-line card, but three per row: 365px instead of 274px. The name keeps a 15px face and the pill beside it, the end cluster keeps every mark, and the figures get a fourth slot for the kind word. The row count you asked for is the one thing this gives up.

  • Least change. The shipped face with wider columns and fixed slots.
  • Room for the kind word. "git project" and "skill" come back to the figures line.
  • Three per row. Twelve cards need four rows instead of three.

Where I would land

D2. It keeps the two-line card and four across, and it fixes all three faults with columns rather than by adding a line: the name gets its own run, the end column holds the marks, the date sits at the end of the figures row at the same x on every card, and the readout slots put every figure under its neighbour's. The filled well for "yours" is the one thing to learn; if you would rather keep the word, D1 says it in text at the cost of a line.
